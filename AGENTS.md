# AGENTS.md: working on Villa Villekulla

Context for AI agents and new contributors. The README covers what the game is and how to play. This file covers what isn't obvious from reading the code. The general recipe (shared with *Hello, IT*, https://github.com/w3nl/it) is in [docs/escape-room-blueprint.md](docs/escape-room-blueprint.md); this file is this project's own notes.

> **Spoilers:** the puzzle solutions are listed below. You'll need them to test changes.

## What this is

*Villa Villekulla* is a point-and-click escape room, an unofficial fan homage to Astrid Lindgren's *Pippi Longstocking* books (Swedish original: *Pippi Långstrump*; Dutch: *Pippi Langkous*, *Villa Kakelbont*). You're a kid visiting Pippi's house. Every puzzle teaches one real idea for children, wrapped in a Pippi joke: in Chapter 0 (Pippi moves in to Villa Villekulla) rust and oil, how a pump lifts water (air pressure), estimating and adding weights, how a mirror bounces light, stacking heavy things at the bottom, and reading a clock; in Chapter 1 (Mr Nilsson has locked you in) mirror writing, sums, the fire triangle, doing steps in the right order, signal flags and feeding a horse safely; in Chapter 2 (burglars in the night) how batteries go in a torch, shadows, finding the North Star, Morse code and a balance scale; in Chapter 3 (Pippi goes to school) Roman numerals, the nines times table, ABC order, insects, fractions and why the seasons happen; in Chapter 4 (a snowball fight with the policemen Kling and Klang) reading animal tracks, temperatures below zero, building a wall in running bond, salt on ice, staying warm in layers and the best angle for a throw; in Chapter 5 (Pippi goes to the circus) money and change, mixing coloured light, levers, patterns, pendulums and gears; in Chapter 6 (Pippi's balloon ride) why hot air rises, steering a balloon by the winds at different heights, maps seen from above, air resistance, what clouds are made of (and why it is colder higher up), and which way a weathercock points; in Chapter 7 (on board the Hoppetossa) pulleys, compass points, floating and sinking, fresh water from sea water, finding the Equator by the North Star, and why the Earth hides far-away islands; in Chapter 8 (the South Sea island) reading a sundial, tides and the Moon, measuring with paces and metres, food chains, how far away a thunderstorm is, and why baby turtles need a dark beach; in Chapter 9 (the voyage home on the Hoppetossa) opposite seasons north and south of the Equator, ocean currents (a message in a bottle), reefing the sails in a storm and tying a reef knot, why swallows fly north, telling lighthouses apart by their flashes, and red and green buoys; in Chapter 10 (home again to Villa Villekulla) tree rings, rolling versus sliding (friction), magnets, why food goes off, where a rainbow appears, and what a hedgehog needs after hibernating.

- 11 chapters so far, numbered 0 to 10: moving day at Villa Villekulla (6 puzzles), the kitchen (6 puzzles), the bedroom at night (6 puzzles), the classroom (6 puzzles), the snowy garden (6 puzzles), the big top (6 puzzles), the balloon ride (6 puzzles), the Hoppetossa (6 puzzles), the South Sea island (6 puzzles), the voyage home (6 puzzles) and the spring garden of Villa Villekulla (6 puzzles). Plain HTML, CSS, JavaScript and SVG: **no build step, no framework, no runtime dependencies** (only Google Fonts).
- Hosted as a static site (GitHub Pages), installable as a PWA, playable offline.
- Three languages: **UK English** (in the code), **Dutch** (`lang/nl/*.txt`) and **Swedish** (`lang/sv/*.txt`). The language button cycles EN → NL → SV.
- **It must be served over http(s)** (`npm start`, http://127.0.0.1:4173); `file://` shows a "needs a web server" message.
- `node_modules/` and `package.json` exist **only for the tests and the local server**.
- Audience: children (about 8–12) and families. Keep words simple, puzzles fair, and nothing scary.

## How the code fits together

- **Classic scripts in one shared global scope**, loaded in order by `boot.js`. Names must be unique across files; chapter-specific names end in the chapter number (`stage1`, `intro1`, `win1`) or say what they are (`openPancake`). Avoid browser globals.
- The scene SVG is in the page before any script runs; `core.js` wires up every `.hit` when it loads. `buildKitchen()` (called from `start()`) draws the flag string, the flag chart and the bureau drawers; `applyKitchen()` shows the state (fire, key, note, padlocks, horse, door light).
- **Hotspot ids** share one namespace across all rooms. Chapter 3 uses: `window3 horse3 board tables romans clock3 stars plaque door3 teacher3 tdesk register bell3 nature globe ta3 pippi3 cake nilsson3 spunk3`. Chapter 4 uses: `snow4 church4 gate4 house4 window4 spunk4 thermo4 horse4 steps4 oak4 birds4 snowman4 tracks4 fort4 ta4 pippi4 police4`. Chapter 5 uses: `booth spunk5 list5 lamps trapeze seal clown curtain winch ringmaster teeter pippi5`. Chapter 6 uses: `sky6 geese6 cloud6 town6 kite6 police6 farms6 saab6 burglars6 tower6 crowd6 harbour6 house6 horse6 balloon6 ringmaster6 nilsson6 pippi6 kids6 spunk6 burner6 basket6 birds6` (several have one rect on the ground and one in the air, shown by leg). - **Chapters:** `S.ch` and `S.scene` (`move`, `kitchen`, `bedroom`, `school`, `winter`, `circus`, `balloon`, `ship`, `island`, `voyage` or `home`) pick the room. Finishing a chapter sets its `outN` flag; `exitLabels()` then labels the exit "on to Chapter N" (the front door on moving day, the kitchen's front door, the bedroom's veranda door, the classroom door, the garden gate, the circus curtain, the harbour behind the church, the island seen from the Hoppetossa, the Hoppetossa in the island's lagoon, the quay at home), and clicking it (or **Continue to Chapter N** on the end screen) runs `startNew()` (Chapter 1), `startCh2()` … `startCh10()`. **New game** starts at Chapter 0 (`startCh0()`), and a visitor without a save sees moving day behind the title. The unlocks are saved as `pippi-villa-v1-ch1` … `-ch10`, which show the chapter buttons on the title screen. `LAST_CH` in `app.js` is the newest chapter.
- **Renumbering:** chapters were added in between three times: winter became Chapter 4 (the circus and the Hoppetossa were 4 and 5), the balloon ride became Chapter 6 (the Hoppetossa, the island and home were 6, 7 and 8), and the voyage home became Chapter 9 (home was 9). `renumber()` in `app.js` moves old unlocks and scores up once (marker `pippi-villa-v1-ord`, now `4`), and `start()` puts an old save in the right chapter by its room (`ROOM_CH`). The test helper `seed()` sets the marker to `4`. Hotspot ids, functions, flags (`outN`), files and translation files were renumbered too.
- **SVG ids are global**: prefix new ones per room. Flags use generated clip-path ids (`fclipN`).

### Languages
- English in the code, translations in `lang/<code>/*.txt` as `= English` / `> translation` pairs (see the blueprint, section 4). Use `tx()` for text built from pieces.
- `js/i18n.js`: `LANGS` and `LANG_TAGS` list the languages; the browser language picks Dutch or Swedish on the first visit. `js/app.js`: `LANG_NEXT` (button order) and `LANG_HELLO` (the line shown after switching, written in the new language). `js/voice.js` speaks `LANG_TAGS[LANG]`.

## Gotchas

Everything in the blueprint's "Lessons learned" applies. In particular:
- `[hidden]{display:none!important}` in `base.css` must stay.
- CSS class names are global; search `css/` first (`.chart`, `.hand`, `.secret`, `.wheel`, `.pstep`… are taken).
- **Motion:** every moving part is a **CSS animation** (no SMIL), so the kill-switch (the last rule in `animations.css`) stops them all for "Motion off" and for devices that ask for reduced motion (unless the player picks "Motion on"). In JS, use `reducedMotion()`. Things that only exist while moving (the bird, the shooting star) start off-screen or invisible, so they disappear when motion is off. `tests/motion.spec.js` checks that nothing in either room moves with Motion off.
  - Moving day (`css/chapter0.css`): clouds drift, birds fly past (they start off-screen), smoke puffs from the chimneys next door and in town, the sun's rays turn, the oak and the grass sway, a squirrel peeps out of the oak's hollow, butterflies and a bumblebee wander over the flowers, a blackbird pecks in its nest on Villa Villekulla's cold chimney, a spider climbs its thread and dust drifts in the empty house, the puddle glints, Pippi's plaits swing, the horse nods, the spunk crawls, water gushes from the pump, and once the clock is set its pendulum swings (`.mv-ticking`). ## Tone and writing

- **Humour first, teaching second.** Every wrong answer gets a joke *and* a nudge.
- Keep the characters:
  - **Pippi:** strongest girl in the world, cheerful, boastful, tells tall tales ("in Egypt everybody…"), wrong but confident maths, catchphrase "I've never done it before, so I'm sure I can" (NL: "Ik heb het nog nooit gedaan, dus ik denk dat ik het wel kan"; SV: "Det har jag aldrig provat förut, så det klarar jag säkert").
  - **Mr Nilsson:** her monkey in blue trousers and a straw hat; says "Eek!" (NL "Iek!", SV "Iii!"); mischievous, loves pancakes.
  - **The horse:** white with black spots, lives on the veranda, Pippi lifts it.
  - **Tommy and Annika:** the polite neighbour children; Annika is clever and a bit anxious.
  - **The spunk:** the free-hint beetle; it says "…".
  - **Thunder-Karlsson and Bloom** (NL Donder-Karlsson en Blom, SV Dunder-Karlsson och Blom): the burglars. Karlsson is big and bossy, Bloom small and jumpy. Silly, never scary.
  - **Kling and Klang** (NL Kling en Klang, SV Kling och Klang): the policemen. Friendly and a bit pompous; Kling is tall and serious (mostly), Klang round and jolly. In Chapter 4 they are the "snowball champions of the whole police station" and good losers.
  - **Miss** (NL de juf, SV fröken): the teacher at the little school. Kind, a bit strict, very fond of her geraniums; secretly delighted by Pippi.
  - **The ringmaster** (NL de directeur, SV direktören): top hat, moustache, panics easily. **The clown**: sniffy, says "Honk!". **The strongman**: big, proud and gentle, until Pippi lifts him. The trapeze act is the Flying Svenssons (Sofia and Sven).
  - **Captain Efraim Longstocking** (NL kapitein Efraïm Langkous, SV kapten Efraim Långstrump): Pippi's father, huge, red-bearded, loud and jolly; king of an island in the South Seas; calls Pippi "Pippilotta" and everybody else "sailor".
- Running jokes: two times three makes four, eighteen kilos of sweets, thing-searching, sleeping with feet on the pillow, the suitcase of gold (and the burglars who want it), pluttification, Pippi coming to school only for the holidays, the old pancake (Chapter 1's, still on the kitchen table in Chapter 9).
- Don't copy text, songs or illustrations from the books or films; write our own and draw our own.
- **New names** (pupils, circus artists, letter writers…): typical Swedish names (Åsa, Liv, Björn, Sven, Per, Britta…), so they fit Pippi's little Swedish town; they stay the same in every language.
- Every puzzle needs two hints in `HINTS`: a nudge, then the answer. Every English line needs a Dutch and a Swedish line.
